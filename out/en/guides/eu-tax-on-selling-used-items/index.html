Do you pay tax on selling used items? Rules in all 27 EU countries

Last updated: 26 November 2026 · SortwellStudios

In almost every EU country, selling your own used items for less than you paid is tax-free. The differences lie in special limits, such as Finland's €5,000 or Lithuania's €2,500 a year, short resale periods in Austria, Germany, Luxembourg and Poland, and in when regular reselling counts as a business.

DAC7 reporting is not a tax

Under DAC7, platforms such as Vinted, eBay and Etsy report sellers who reach 30 sales or more than €2,000 on that platform in a calendar year. That report only tells the tax office what you sold. Whether you owe tax depends on your country's rules below, and on whether you sell your own things or buy goods to resell at a profit. Buying to resell regularly counts as a business in every EU country.

The table covers individuals selling their own used personal items (clothes, furniture, books, toys). Checked against official or specialist sources on 26 November 2026.

What to do

  • Keep a simple list of what you buy and sell, with prices and fees.
  • If you buy to resell, register the activity as your country requires.
  • If a letter arrives after a DAC7 report, read Letter from the tax office after a DAC7 report.
