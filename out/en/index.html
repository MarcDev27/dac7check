DAC7 in plain words

DAC7 is an EU rule that requires online platforms to report their sellers' data to the tax authorities. It has applied in every EU country since 2023. It is a reporting rule, not a new tax.

You are reported if, on one platform in one calendar year, you make 30 or more sales or more than €2,000. Either limit is enough. The platform then asks for your tax ID and sends your data by 31 January for the previous year.

Frequently asked questions

When does Vinted report my sales to the tax office?

As soon as you reach 30 sales or go over €2,000 on Vinted in one calendar year. The same applies to eBay, Etsy and other platforms covered by DAC7, each one separately.

Do I have to pay tax if I'm reported?

Not automatically. Occasionally selling your own used things is usually treated as private. Buying items specifically to resell at a profit can count as a business, and then tax and other obligations may apply. The details depend on your country.

Do my sales on different platforms add up?

Not for the report. Each platform only looks at the sales made on it. For your taxes, though, your total income counts, whichever platform it came from.

Are the limits changing soon?

In 2026 the European Commission proposed raising the limit for sales of goods to €3,000 and dropping the 30-sales rule. That would apply from 2028 at the earliest and has not been adopted yet. Use the switch in the check to see what it would change for you.
